## Is Tailor Brands Legit?

Yes. Let's clear this up first because it's the most-searched question.

- **Founded:** 2014. Started as an AI logo and branding platform, added LLC formation later — important context for understanding what the product actually is.
- **What it is:** an authorized business-filing service that can legally form an LLC in all 50 states.
- **Operating reach:** all 50 states.
- **Reviews:** generally positive customer ratings on Trustpilot, with a recurring set of complaints about auto-renewals and surprise fees (verify the current score on Trustpilot).

Tailor Brands is not a scam. It is not fly-by-night. It will file your LLC and the LLC will be valid. The problem isn't legitimacy — it's fit. Tailor Brands is a branding company first and a formation service second. For a landlord forming a rental LLC, that ordering is backwards.

Plain English: "is Tailor Brands legit" and "is Tailor Brands the right tool for my rental LLC" are two different questions. The answer to the first is yes. The answer to the second is usually no.

## What Tailor Brands Actually Does

Tailor Brands' product mix, in roughly the order the company cares about it:

- **Branding suite** — AI logo maker, brand book, website builder, online store, digital business cards, social-media post maker. This is the core product and where the company makes its margin.
- **LLC formation** in all 50 states (the part you came for).
- **Registered agent service** — a separate annual add-on, not bundled into the base price.
- **EIN application service** — extra fee (free if you DIY at irs.gov).
- **Operating agreement** — included on Essential and up; an add-on otherwise.
- **Annual compliance / annual report filing** — extra service.
- **Business licenses, bookkeeping, and other add-ons** surfaced through the checkout funnel.

Notice the shape of it. The formation is the hook; the branding suite and the recurring subscription are the business. For a software founder launching a consumer brand, that bundle can make sense. For a landlord who needs a liability wrapper around a duplex, you're paying for a logo studio you'll never open.

## Tailor Brands Pricing in Detail

Pricing as of 2026 — verify on tailorbrands.com before purchasing, because the tiers and promotional discounts change often.

### The three formation plans

| Plan | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Lite | ~$0 + state fee | Basic LLC filing, name check. Standard (slow) processing. |
| Essential | ~$199/yr + state fee | Adds operating agreement, expedited processing, business tools. |
| Elite | ~$249/yr + state fee | Adds 1st-year registered agent, priority support, branding kit (logo, website, domain, business card). |

The number that matters most: **these are annual subscriptions, not one-time fees.** Essential and Elite bill again every year unless you cancel. That's the single biggest thing landlords miss.

### The add-ons that stack up

- **Registered agent:** ~$199/yr. On Lite and Essential it's a separate add-on; on Elite the first year is included, then it renews at ~$199/yr. This is roughly $74/yr more than [Northwest's $125/yr standalone RA](/llc-guide/northwest-registered-agent-review).
- **EIN:** ~$70. Free if you do it yourself at irs.gov/ein in about 10 minutes.
- **Annual report filing:** extra charge on top of the state's own fee.
- **Rush / expedited processing:** extra on Lite and Essential; the free Lite plan can take around two weeks before your documents are even submitted to the state.

### What it actually costs

The advertised "$0" Lite plan is technically accurate and practically misleading. Once you add registered agent service and an EIN — the two things almost every real LLC needs — the realistic first-year all-in lands somewhere around **$270-$380+ before state filing fees**, per multiple 2026 pricing breakdowns. Add an operating agreement and a state fee and you're well past what a flat-fee competitor charges.

The real number: budget several hundred dollars for the first year and an ongoing annual subscription after that — not the "free" the landing page implies.

## Tailor Brands Pros — What It Does Well

To be fair, there are real reasons Tailor Brands exists and sells well.

### 1. Genuine all-in-one for new brands

If you are launching a business that actually needs a logo, a website, a domain, business cards, and social assets — and you want LLC formation in the same checkout — Tailor Brands legitimately bundles all of it. For a DTC e-commerce founder or a personal-brand consultant, the Elite plan can be cheaper than buying those pieces separately.

### 2. Polished, modern interface

The website and dashboard are slick. Compared to the dated UI of some operator-grade formation services, Tailor Brands feels current and is easy to navigate. The onboarding wizard is genuinely well-designed.

### 3. Real filing service in all 50 states

The formation itself is legitimate and nationwide. Your articles of organization get filed, your LLC is valid, and you get the documents. There is nothing fake about the core service.

### 4. Responsive to complaints

Reviews note that when customers raise issues — including surprise fees — Tailor Brands' support tends to respond and will often issue refunds or credits when the complaint is reasonable. That's a point in its favor, even if it's a point you'd rather not need.

## Tailor Brands Cons — What's Frustrating (For Landlords)

This is the credibility section. Here's where Tailor Brands costs landlords money.

### 1. The recurring-subscription model is the whole problem

Your rental LLC is a one-time setup with an ongoing registered-agent need. It is not a SaaS product. Tailor Brands' Essential and Elite plans are annual subscriptions that auto-renew every year — even after your LLC is formed and you have no use for the branding tools you paid for. Customers regularly report being surprised by the renewal charge a year later and finding cancellation harder than signup.

For a landlord, this is the opposite of what you want. You want to form the entity, pay a predictable RA renewal, and not think about it. A subscription that quietly rebills is operational friction you'll pay for in year two, year three, year four.

### 2. The upsell funnel is aggressive

Tailor Brands' checkout walks you through screen after screen, each presenting an add-on as "recommended." It is easy to leave with $100-$300+ in extras you didn't intend to buy — EIN, registered agent, compliance, business tools — stacked onto a "free" formation. This is the single most common complaint across reviews, and it's a design choice, not an accident.

If you do buy Tailor Brands, go in knowing exactly what you need and decline everything else.

### 3. Registered agent is a $199/yr add-on, not included

For a landlord, the registered agent is the part that matters long-term — it's who receives your lawsuits and state notices. Tailor Brands charges ~$199/yr for it as a separate line item (free first year only on Elite). That's more expensive than dedicated RA services, and it's the function Tailor Brands cares about least. You're buying a long-term, mission-critical service from a company whose heart is in logo design.

### 4. EIN is an upsell on a free task

Tailor Brands charges ~$70 to get your EIN. The IRS gives EINs away for free in about 10 minutes at irs.gov/ein. Paying $70 for it is paying for the convenience of not clicking through one government form. For most landlords, skip it.

### 5. You're paying for branding a rental LLC doesn't need

A rental-property LLC does not need a logo, a brand book, a social-media post maker, or a digital business card. "Riverview Holdings LLC" does not market itself on Instagram. Every dollar of the Elite plan that goes to the branding suite is a dollar wasted for a landlord. That's not a knock on the branding tools — they're just irrelevant to the job.

## When Tailor Brands Is Actually the Right Call

We don't recommend Tailor Brands for a rental LLC. But to be fair and useful, here's when it genuinely makes sense — including for some investors:

- **You're building a consumer-facing brand.** If you run short-term rentals under a branded name (a boutique Airbnb operation, a property-management company with a public identity), and you actually want a logo, website, and marketing assets, the Elite bundle can be efficient.
- **You want one checkout for everything.** If the convenience of formation + branding + website in a single flow is worth a premium to you, Tailor Brands delivers that.
- **You'll use the subscription.** If you'll actively use the website builder and branding tools, the recurring fee buys something. If you won't, it doesn't.

For a standard buy-and-hold landlord forming "Property Holdings LLC," none of these apply. For a branded hospitality or property-management business, some might. Know which one you are.

## Tailor Brands for REI-Specific Patterns

The structures landlords actually use, and how Tailor Brands handles them:

### Single rental LLC

The most common case. Tailor Brands will file it, but you're overpaying for the formation and signing up for a recurring subscription to do something that should be a one-time task. Northwest does this cleaner and cheaper. See [how to start an LLC for rental property](/llc-guide/how-to-start-an-llc-for-rental-property) for the step-by-step.

### Multi-property portfolio (one LLC per property)

This is where the subscription model hurts most. Multiply an annual subscription and a ~$199/yr RA across five or ten LLCs and the cumulative cost balloons fast — and you've got multiple auto-renewals to track. A flat-fee formation with a cheaper RA renewal is dramatically more economical at portfolio scale.

### Wyoming holding LLC + property-state registration

Privacy-conscious investors often form a Wyoming holding LLC, then foreign-qualify in the property state. This is a privacy play — and privacy is exactly where a branding-first company is the wrong fit. You want a service whose default is keeping your name off public records, not one whose default is helping you broadcast a brand. Northwest's Privacy by Default is built for this; Tailor Brands isn't positioned for it.

### DSCR loan readiness

DSCR lenders ask for clean entity documents at underwriting — articles of organization, operating agreement, EIN letter, member list. Tailor Brands can produce these, but the operating agreement is gated behind Essential+ and the EIN is a paid add-on, so you'll pay to assemble the standard lender packet. The documents themselves are fine; you just pay more to get the full set.

## Customer Service and Cancellation Reality

What you actually experience with Tailor Brands:

- **Support:** modern, multi-channel, and generally responsive. When customers escalate fee complaints, support often resolves them with a refund or credit. That's genuinely a positive.
- **The friction point:** cancellation. The most consistent complaint is the auto-renewal and the effort required to stop it. Set a calendar reminder before your renewal date if you sign up, and check exactly what's set to rebill.
- **Refund policy:** Tailor Brands does resolve many complaints with refunds case-by-case, but a clear, published blanket refund policy for formation services isn't consistently documented across reviews. Confirm the current refund terms directly with Tailor Brands before purchasing rather than assuming — this is a "read the fine print" situation.

## Final Verdict — Should You Use Tailor Brands for Your Rental LLC?

**For most landlords: no.** Tailor Brands is a legitimate, well-built branding company that files LLCs as a side product. For a rental-property LLC — which needs filing, a registered agent, and an operating agreement, and nothing else — you'll overpay, get pushed through an upsell funnel, and end up on a recurring subscription for a one-time job. The branding suite that justifies the price is exactly the thing a rental LLC doesn't use.

**For a branded hospitality or property-management business that actually wants marketing assets:** the Elite bundle can pencil out. Know that's you before you buy.

**For the standard buy-and-hold rental LLC:** use a flat-fee, registered-agent-first service. That's [Northwest Registered Agent](/llc-guide/northwest-registered-agent-review) for our money — cheaper, cleaner, more private, no subscription trap.
