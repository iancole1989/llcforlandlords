## How We Evaluated These Services for Real Estate Investors

Most "best LLC service" lists rank by what matters to a startup founder: brand recognition, marketing UX, total checkout friction. For a real-estate investor, different things matter. Our criteria:

1. **Registered agent quality.** Privacy, mail forwarding speed, scan/upload reliability. RA is the LLC's legal point of contact — quality matters for years, not just at formation.

2. **Pricing transparency.** No manipulative upsells. Clear "this is what you're paying for" pricing. No hidden recurring charges.

3. **Series LLC support.** Series LLCs are a portfolio-investor structure (TX, DE, IL, NV, WY). Some services handle them well; some don't.

4. **Operating agreement quality.** REI-specific clauses (capital calls for renovation, distribution waterfall, transfer restrictions) vs generic startup-founder boilerplate.

5. **Out-of-state filing competence.** Wyoming holding LLC + foreign registration in property state is a common REI pattern. The service needs to handle both filings cleanly.

6. **Customer support when something complex happens.** Deed transfer questions. Lender questions. BOI filing questions. Series LLC questions. Mass-market services often punt on these; quality services don't.

7. **Compatibility with REI tooling.** Banking partners (Relay, Bluevine), bookkeeping integrations (Stessa, Baselane).

## What an LLC Formation Service Actually Delivers (And What It Doesn't)

This section is here because internet content tends to oversell what these services do. The honest read:

### What you're paying for

When you use Northwest, ZenBusiness, or LegalZoom for $39-$300, you're paying for:

- **Form preparation and filing** with the state. They take the data you provide and submit the articles of organization on your behalf. The state still does its own processing on its own timeline.
- **Registered agent service** for year 1 (in most service tiers). The RA is the legal point of contact for service of process — which matters every year, not just at formation.
- **A generic operating agreement template.** Useful starting point. Not REI-specialized.
- **Compliance reminders** in some tiers. Annual report deadline emails, franchise tax due-date warnings.
- **Customer support** when you have a question (variable quality across services).

### What you're NOT paying for

- **Legal advice.** None of these services give you legal advice. The customer service teams will help with formation logistics; they will not help with "should I structure as a series LLC" or "should I include drag-along rights" — those are attorney questions.
- **Tax advice.** Same. Your CPA handles tax structuring.
- **A finished operating agreement.** The templates are a starting point. For multi-member or property over $500k, supplement with attorney review.
- **A guarantee that your LLC will hold up in court.** The veil depends on you operating the LLC as a separate entity — separate bank account, no commingling, signed operating agreement, maintained annual reports. The service files your formation; the rest is on you.

### The DIY math

For a confident operator on a 2nd or 3rd LLC, DIY is real. The state filing fee is the same whether you use a service or not. The forms are filled in identically. The time investment is 4-8 hours.

Where the service earns its $39-$300:
- First-time filers who don't know what to put on the articles of organization.
- Multi-state filings (Wyoming holding LLC + foreign-qualification) where a service that knows the cross-state nuances saves real time.
- Operators who value time over $50-$200 and just want the formation done.

For everyone else, DIY is the cheapest path. Use a $50-150/yr standalone RA service (Northwest's standalone RA is $125/yr) and skip the formation service entirely.

## REI-Specific Banking and Bookkeeping (Adjacent to Formation)

Once your LLC is formed, the next operational steps tie directly to which formation service you used — or didn't. Quick survey of what we use and recommend, beyond the formation service itself:

### Business banking for REI

- **Relay** — free business checking, REI-friendly, supports 20+ sub-accounts (one per property). Online-only. Works for LLCs of any size; particularly good for portfolio operators tracking per-property cash flow.
- **Bluevine** — free business checking with interest. Good ACH and wire functionality. Online-only.
- **Mercury** — digital-first, fast onboarding, no monthly fees. No cash deposits — matters if you collect cash from tenants.
- **Local credit union or community bank** — relationship-driven lending. Useful when you need a portfolio loan, HELOC against your primary, or a banker who knows your operation. Slower onboarding but pays off if you're scaling.

### Bookkeeping software for REI

- **Stessa** — free, REI-focused, automatically categorizes transactions, generates Schedule E reports. Best for landlords managing 1-10 properties personally.
- **Baselane** — REI banking + bookkeeping integrated. Useful if you don't already have a separate bank.
- **QuickBooks Online** — generic but powerful. Works for any portfolio size. More setup work than Stessa but more flexibility.

ZenBusiness has banking partnerships (Bluevine, Relay) accessible during checkout — convenient if you're forming the LLC and opening business banking simultaneously. Northwest doesn't have built-in banking partnerships; you'll set up banking separately. LegalZoom has some banking partnerships but they're not REI-focused.

For most REI: form the LLC with Northwest (or whichever service fits), then set up Relay banking + Stessa bookkeeping. The formation service is one decision; the banking and bookkeeping stack is a separate decision and matters more for ongoing operations.

## State-Specific Considerations

### Best LLC Service in Texas

For most TX LLC formations: **Northwest**. Texas has $300 state filing fee and no annual report fee under the franchise threshold ($1.23M revenue). For series LLCs (TX is one of the leading series-LLC states), Northwest handles series filings cleanly.

### Best LLC Service in Florida

For most FL LLC formations: **Northwest**. FL has $125 filing fee, $138.75/yr annual report (due May 1 — Northwest sends reminders). For Florida transfer-in scenarios, watch the documentary stamp tax (Fla. Stat. § 201.02) on transfers from individual to wholly-owned LLC.

### Best LLC Service in Wyoming

For WY LLC formations: **Northwest**. They originated as a WY-focused agent and know WY filings cold. WY allows anonymous LLCs (no member names on public record) — Northwest handles the privacy stack natively. WY filing fee is $100, annual report $60.

### Best LLC Service in Montana

For MT LLC formations: **Northwest**. Montana is straightforward — $35 filing fee, $20/yr annual report.

## What to Do Before You Click Buy

Three checks before you commit to a service:

### 1. Verify current pricing on the service's site

Pricing in any "best of" article — including this one — ages. Northwest, ZenBusiness, and LegalZoom update pricing tiers, RA fees, and add-on costs frequently. Before you check out, verify on the service's pricing page that what you're seeing matches what you read here. We update this article quarterly, but we cannot guarantee it's accurate to the day.

### 2. Read the privacy policy section on data sharing

Especially relevant for ZenBusiness Starter tier (data sharing with marketing partners) and any "free formation" tier across all services. The privacy treatment is in the privacy policy, not the marketing pages — read it before checkout.

### 3. Understand the year 2+ cost, not just year 1

The formation fee gets the headlines. The recurring registered agent fee is what you actually pay over the life of the LLC. Northwest $125/yr, ZenBusiness $199/yr, LegalZoom $249/yr. Over 5 years, the difference is meaningful — Northwest is roughly $625 cheaper than LegalZoom across the period.

For long-term LLCs (which is most REI LLCs — you'll hold the entity as long as you hold the property), the annual cost matters more than the one-time formation cost.
